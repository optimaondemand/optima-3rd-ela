This Week at a Glance

The book ends this week. The outlaws stop Captain Nips’ coach and grab both boys. When they ask where the whipping boy went, the prince answers, “Swam the river.” Billy whips the prince, and the prince makes no sound, just as Jemmy never did. Betsy’s bear, Petunia, chases the outlaws off. In the city, the boys walk through a fair, where the prince carries a kettle for the first time and calls himself “Friend-O’-Jemmy.” A news seller shouts that the king wants the whipping boy caught, so Jemmy runs for the sewers, and the prince follows. The outlaws run into the brewery rats. Back at the castle, the prince promises to do his lessons and behave so that Jemmy can stay under his protection.

Reading skill
Finding the theme. In the last chapters, notice what the characters have learned, and say the story’s lesson in one sentence.
Big idea
Friendship and change. The boys who started as enemies end the book as friends. Which boy changed the most?

“So help me, if it’s a friend you ran off looking for, it’s a friend you found!”Jemmy’s thought, Chapter 20

Word parts
Five parts that go at the front of a word: re (again), un (not), pre (before), dis (not), mis (wrong).rebuild · unhappy · preview · disagree · misread
Spelling
Homophones sound the same but mean different things, so the spelling shows which one you mean.to · two · too · their · there · they’re · its · it’s
Grammar
A prepositional phrase starts with a preposition and ends on a noun. It tells where, when, or how.through the fog · before sunrise · with great care

New in the Lessons

🕯️ Out of the Sewer
This week’s word game. Each question is a fork with two tunnels. The right tunnel lights a lamp and moves Jemmy and the prince closer to daylight; a wrong one gets a squeak from the brewery rats. Eight lamps a day, until the boys reach the river on Day 4.
🎬 Opti’s videos
Every Word Study activity, all four days, starts with a short video where Opti shows how it works. On Day 2, Opti also shows how to do Read Like a Writer with a different sentence from the book.
🔍 Reading lens
Before reading, your student taps one of two lenses to use, like “What has someone learned?” It shows as a bookmark in the reading box.
🦁 Courage Watch
After reading, your student decides if three moments from the chapters show courage: Yes or Not yet.

Each day opens with a real old painting or print: a dancing bear in a village, a London fair from 1733, a palace throne room, and a sailing ship. Your student looks first, then taps to learn about it.

Writer’s Workshop

This week your student compares Jemmy and Prince Brat. Day 1: fill in a character change chart for each boy (beginning, end, what changed him). Day 2: write an opening sentence that names both boys. Day 3: write one way they changed the same and one way they changed differently. Day 4: write a conclusion and one sentence that says what the story teaches.

An example writer named Clara shows each step first.

Ask at Home

  1. What did the prince do when Billy whipped him?Listen for: he set his jaw and made no sound, just like Jemmy always did.
  2. What new name did the prince give himself at the fair?Listen for: “Friend-O’-Jemmy.”
  3. Why did the prince wink at Jemmy?Listen for: turning Jemmy in was a trick to get him safely back to the castle.
  4. What is the lesson of The Whipping Boy, in one sentence?Listen for: an idea about friendship, fairness, or people changing, with one event as proof. Many answers can be right.

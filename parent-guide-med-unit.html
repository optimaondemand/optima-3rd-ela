About the Unit

Your student just finished The Whipping Boy, a made-up story set long ago. For the next two weeks, your student reads true articles about real life in the Middle Ages: castles, servants and apprentices, royal courts, food, how castles were built, how a boy became a knight, busy medieval towns, and what stories get right and wrong about the past.

Each lesson has one short article right on the page, so there is no book to have nearby. We read these articles because they practice the reading skills nonfiction needs (finding the central idea and spotting cause and effect), and because they help students see what in The Whipping Boy came from history and what the author made up.

“Stories borrow from history but add imagination.”Real vs. Imagined, Week 24 Day 4

How a Week Works

Days 1–4
Read one short article, practice the week’s word study (a word part, a spelling pattern, a grammar skill and new vocabulary), answer three questions and take one step in the informational writing.
Job Spotlight
Each day meets one real medieval job, like the page, the apprentice, the stonemason or the scribe.
Diligence Watch
After reading, your student decides if three moments show someone working hard and carefully: Yes or Not yet.
Check-In
A short Canvas quiz at the end of each week on that week’s skills and articles.

Your Student’s Informational Writing

Writer’s Workshop
Wk 23 · Choose & take notesWk 24 · Draft, revise & publish

Across the two weeks, your student writes an informational paragraph about one small part of medieval life. Week 23: choose a topic that is not too big, write a research question, take notes from two articles, and plan the paragraph (topic sentence, two details, closing). Week 24: draft it, revise so every sentence fits the topic, edit for capitals and periods, and publish. An example writer named Clara shows each step first.

Good to Know Ahead of Time

Hard lives
The articles are honest that life was hard for most people: servants worked long days, children often learned a job by working instead of going to school, and poor families ate plain food. Nothing is graphic.
Rank and fairness
The royal court article explains strict rules about rank. In The Whipping Boy, the prince does whatever he wants without punishment, and the lessons ask students whether that was realistic.
Old jobs
The Job Spotlight on the barber-surgeon explains that medieval barbers also pulled bad teeth and treated cuts. “Stories say” the barber pole’s stripes come from that old job.
Real old pictures
The lessons use real paintings and book pages from the Middle Ages and later, so the pictures your student sees are from history.

How to Help (Without Doing It for Them)

Try this

  • Ask “What was the most important point of today’s article?” and then “What fact proves it?”
  • Ask “Why?” about something in the article, and listen for a because sentence.
  • Point them to Opti’s tips and the hint buttons before you step in.
  • Be the audience for the published paragraph.

Try to avoid

  • Typing or wording answers for them. Teachers need to see your student’s own thinking.
  • Fixing their writing. Revising and editing are lessons of their own in Week 24.
  • Choosing the writing topic for them. A topic they care about makes the writing easier.

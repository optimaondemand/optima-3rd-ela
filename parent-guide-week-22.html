This Week at a Glance

Your student finished The Whipping Boy last week, so there are no new chapters. This week is for looking back. Day 1 compares how Jemmy and the prince changed from the first chapter to the last. Day 2 names what the story teaches, in one sentence that would fit anyone. Day 3 turns the book into a play: your student picks a scene and reads it out loud with a voice for each character. Day 4 is for sharing: your student reads the finished comparison paragraph out loud, sums up the whole book, and looks back on it.

Reading skill
Looking back at a whole book. Compare how characters changed, name the theme, and read a scene with expression.
Big idea
Friendship and change. The boys who started as enemies end the book as friends. What does the story teach about people changing?

“If you boys decide to run away again, take me with you.”The king, Chapter 20

Words to Know

transformto change completely
compareto look at two things side by side for sameness and difference
contrastto point out the differences between two things
developto grow and change over time
themethe big message or lesson of a story
evidencea detail from the book that proves your idea
resolutionhow the problem is settled at the end
character developmenthow a character grows and changes
expressionshowing feelings with your voice, face, or body
dialoguethe words characters say
fluencyreading smoothly at the right speed
prosodythe rhythm and stress of your voice
reflectto think carefully about something that happened
celebrateto mark something with joy
novela long story with characters, plot and theme
memorableworth remembering
Word parts
The root struct means build. It hides inside many long words.construct · structure · instruct · construction
Spelling
A compound word is two small words joined into one. A long word is not always a compound word.mailbox · postcard · sidewalk · railroad
Grammar
A pronoun takes the place of a name. Some do the action (he, they), some get it (him, them), and some show whose (his, their).He handed them his map.

New in the Lessons

🏰 Build the Castle
This week’s word game, because struct means build. Each right answer lays one stone in a castle wall, from the bottom row up. Eight stones a day; on Day 4 the castle gets its towers, gate and flag.
🎬 Opti’s videos
Every Word Study activity, all four days, starts with a short video where Opti shows how it works. On Day 2, Opti also shows how to do Read Like a Writer with a different sentence from the book.
🔍 Reading lens
Before looking back, your student taps one of two lenses to use, like “Look for the lesson.” It shows as a bookmark in the Looking Back box.
🦁 Courage Watch
After looking back, your student decides if three moments from across the book show courage: Yes or Not yet.

Each day opens with a real old painting or print: a young prince of Spain on horseback, two street boys sharing a meal, a laughing audience at a play from 1733, and printers at work in 1568. Your student looks first, then taps to learn about it.

Ask at Home

  1. Who changed more, Jemmy or the prince? How do you know?Listen for: a detail from early in the book and one from the end. Either boy can be the answer.
  2. What does The Whipping Boy teach, in one sentence?Listen for: a life lesson that fits anyone, not just what happened.
  3. Which scene did you read out loud? How did each character sound?Listen for: a chapter, and a feeling for each voice (bossy, scared, sure of herself).
  4. What does the word part struct mean? Can you name a word with it?Listen for: build, and a word like construct or structure.

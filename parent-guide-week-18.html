This Week at a Glance

A new book! Prince Brat plays pranks, but he is never punished. It is forbidden to spank a prince, so a boy from the streets named Jemmy is kept in the castle to be whipped in his place. One night the bored prince runs away and makes Jemmy come along. By dawn they are lost in a thick fog, and two outlaws, Hold-Your-Nose Billy and Cutwater, grab them. Then the outlaws spot the king’s crest on the saddle.

Reading skill
Noticing who has power. Who gives the orders, who has to obey, and what each character chooses to do about it.
Big idea
Is it fair? One boy has everything and never gets in trouble. The other gets in trouble for things he didn’t do.

ransommoney demanded to free a prisoner
gallowsa frame used for hanging people long ago
Word parts
inter- means “between.” sub- means “under.”interrupt · international · submarine
Spelling
Vowel teams. Two vowels that make one sound stay together in one syllable.rain·bow · sea·son · pea·nut
Grammar
Joining words. and adds, but shows a difference, so shows a result.He was scared, but he kept going.

New in the Lessons

Starting this week, the lesson pages have a guide and a few extras:

🦉 Opti
Opti the owl welcomes your student each day and gives a short tip at the top of each tab. Every message can be heard or read.
🏰 Up the North Tower
This week’s word game. Jemmy’s room is at the top of the castle’s north tower. Each right answer lights one step, 8 a day, until your student reaches his window on Day 4.
💬 Jemmy’s Street Talk
Jemmy grew up on the streets and talks that way (“Ain’t I already been whipped…”). On Day 1, your student taps his words to see them in everyday English.
🛡️ The King’s Crest
On Day 3, your student holds up a lantern to see the kind of royal crest the outlaws find on the saddle, then can design a crest of their own and save it as a picture.

Each day also opens with a real old painting or print that fits the chapter, like a royal feast, a foggy forest and a castle tower, with a note explaining what it shows.

Your Student’s Opinion Writing

Writer’s Workshop
Wk 18 · Choose a claimWk 19 · Add reasonsWk 20 · DraftWk 21 · Revise & publishWk 22 · Compare & present

Your student picks a character and writes an opinion about them. Day 1: a claim (name a character, make a judgment, hint at why). Day 2: one reason. Day 3: a text example that names the chapter. Day 4: a check that the claim, reason and example all do their jobs.

An example writer named Clara shows each step first with her own opinion about Prince Brat.

💬

Ask at Home

  1. What is a whipping boy, and why does the prince have one?Listen for: no one is allowed to spank a prince, so Jemmy is punished instead.
  2. Why won’t Jemmy cry when he gets whacked?Listen for: he doesn’t want to give the prince the fun of hearing him.
  3. Why did the prince run away, and why did he bring Jemmy?Listen for: he was bored; Jemmy thinks it’s because the prince has no friends.
  4. Who do you think has the harder life, Jemmy or the prince?Listen for: an opinion plus a reason from the book. Either answer can be right.

Try at Home

  • Play “and, but, so”: one person says half a sentence, the other finishes it with a joining word
  • Hunt for vowel teams (ai, ea, oa) on a cereal box or a store sign
  • Ask: “Would you take the blame for someone else? When?”

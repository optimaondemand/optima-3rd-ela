✏️ Grammar: Dialogue punctuation review
Define — practice the convention

This week: Dialogue punctuation review

📌 The Rule

When writing dialogue: (1) Put quotation marks around the exact words spoken. (2) The first word of speech gets a capital letter. (3) Punctuation — comma, period, ?, ! — goes INSIDE the closing quotation mark. (4) The dialogue tag (he said, she asked) comes outside the quotation marks.

"Mr Tumnus is in trouble," said Lucy.

Opening quotation mark before Mr; capital M; comma inside the closing quotation mark; the dialogue tag said Lucy follows outside with a period.

🎮

Click the word that does NOT match the correct pattern.

👀 Watch How — Clara, our example writer

Clara begins a new literary response about theme. Today she brainstorms possible themes.

BEFORE
Clara's rough idea: The theme is about being good.
AFTER
Clara's brainstorm: Possible themes — (1) The story shows that sacrifice can free someone from the consequences of their own choices. (2) The story shows that love is more powerful than law. Notice: Clara identified multiple possible themes and then chose the one with the most evidence across the whole book. A theme should be stated as a complete sentence, not just a single word like 'redemption.'

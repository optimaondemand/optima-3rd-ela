This Week at a Glance

In Chapter 7, a beaver leads the children through the snowy wood to his house on a dam, and whispers a name: Aslan. Each child feels something different when they hear it. In Chapter 8, after dinner, Mr. Beaver tells them about Aslan and an old prophecy about four thrones, and then they find that Edmund has slipped away. Chapter 9 follows Edmund alone through the snow to the White Witch’s castle, full of stone statues. Day 1 reads Chapter 7, Day 2 Chapter 8, Day 3 Chapter 9, and Day 4 looks back at all three.

Reading skill
Different characters, different views. Good readers ask what each character wants, fears, or knows, and why two characters can see the same moment so differently.
Big idea
Why do two people in the same situation see things so differently? Lucy and Edmund hear the same name and feel opposite things.

“They say Aslan is on the move.”Mr. Beaver, Chapter 7

Words to Know

Use them at home!
authoritythe right or power to give orders and make decisions
prophecya statement that something will happen in the future
betrayto be disloyal to someone who trusted you
treacherybetraying someone’s trust in a very serious way
Word parts
The word parts in- and im- both mean not. Im- goes before words that start with m or p.impossible · incorrect · impatient · invisible
Spelling
A prefix goes on the front of a base word, and the base word keeps its spelling. Peel the prefix off to read the base.un·happy · re·build · pre·heat · dis·appear
Grammar
Irregular verbs don’t add -ed for the past. You learn each one.go → went · run → ran · eat → ate · tell → told

New in the Lessons

🐟 Mr. Beaver’s Ice Hole
This week’s word game is Mr. Beaver’s fishing hole in the ice. On Days 1 and 2 your student decides whether a word takes in- or im- to mean not. On Days 3 and 4 they keep the words that have a prefix and a real base word, and throw the others back. Each right answer lands a fish on the ice, and by Day 4 the catch is complete.

Each day opens the Reading tab with a real thing behind the story: real beavers and their dams, ice fishing (and staying safe on ice), stone statues, and how the full moon lights up snow.
🏮 Truth Watch
After reading, your student decides if three moments show someone being honest: Yes or Not yet. This week includes Edmund’s secret, the excuses he tells himself, and the people who tell hard truths.

Each day opens with real art, never pictures from the book: two beavers from the Audubons’ book of North American animals (1840s), Pieter Bruegel’s village skating on a frozen river (1565), an old colored photograph of Neuschwanstein Castle in Germany (1890s), and Arkhip Kuindzhi’s moonlight on a snowy forest (about 1900). Your Student’s Writing

Writer’s Workshop
Wk 26 · Explain a character’s choiceWk 27 · Compare two characters’ perspectives

This week’s short response compares how two characters see Aslan or Narnia, and why. Day 1: plan (two characters, how each one feels, and the big difference). Day 2: draft in full sentences with words like shows and reveals. Day 3: revise one vague phrase into precise words. Day 4: publish the final response, ending with what the difference shows.

An example writer named Clara shows each step first.

Ask at Home

  1. How did each child feel when Mr. Beaver said “Aslan”?Listen for: Peter brave, Susan like music floating by, Lucy like the start of summer, Edmund horror.
  2. Why do you think Edmund felt so differently from Lucy?Listen for: Edmund has already sided with the Witch; any answer with a reason from the book.
  3. What did Edmund tell himself on his way to the Witch’s house? Was it true?Listen for: that she was the rightful Queen and wouldn’t hurt them; deep down he knew she was bad.
  4. What does “impossible” mean? Why im- and not in-?Listen for: not possible; possible starts with p.

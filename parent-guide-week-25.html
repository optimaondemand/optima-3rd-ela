This Week at a Glance

A new novel starts this week: The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe by C.S. Lewis. Four children are sent to stay in a big old house in the country. Lucy, the youngest, steps into a wardrobe full of fur coats, and keeps walking, into a snowy wood called Narnia. She meets a Faun named Mr. Tumnus. When she comes back, nobody believes her, until Edmund finds his own way in. Day 1 reads Chapter 1, Day 2 reads Chapters 2 and 3, and Days 3 and 4 look back at all three chapters. Please have the book ready.

Reading skill
How an author builds a world. Good readers slow down in a fantasy story and notice the details: the weather, the light, and what could never happen at home.
Big idea
What makes a made-up world feel real? Lewis uses things you can feel, hear and see: snow that is soft and powdery and extremely cold.

“This must be a simply enormous wardrobe!”Lucy, Chapter 1

Words to Know

Use them at home!
inquisitiveeager to find out; very curious
melancholya deep feeling of sadness or gloom
peculiarstrange or unusual in a hard-to-explain way
betrayto be disloyal to someone who trusted you
Word parts
The word part tele means far.telephone · television · telescope · telegram
Spelling
The schwa is the lazy uh sound in a quiet beat of a long word. Any vowel can make it, which is why long words are hard to spell by ear.a·bout · les·son · pen·cil · cir·cus
Grammar
A complex sentence has a main idea and a part that can’t stand alone, joined by a word like when, because or although. When that part comes first, a comma follows it.When Lucy stepped through the fur coats, she felt snow under her shoes.

New in the Lessons

🧥 Through the Wardrobe
This week’s word game is the inside of a wardrobe full of fur coats. Each coat has a word with a lazy uh beat. Your student says the word and taps the vowel that makes the uh. Each right answer pushes a coat aside, so more of the snowy wood shows through, and the lamp-post shines brighter every day.
🎬 Opti’s videos
Every Word Study activity, all four days, starts with a short video where Opti shows how it works. On Day 2, Opti also shows Read Like a Writer with a different sentence from Chapter 3. The videos have captions for now; Opti’s voice will be added later.
🌍 World Notes
Each day opens the Reading tab with a real thing behind the story: children sent away from London in World War II, fauns in very old stories, gas lamp-posts, and reindeer that pull sledges.
🏮 Truth Watch
After reading, your student decides if three moments from the chapters show someone being honest: Yes or Not yet. Honesty is the trait for the whole Narnia unit.

Each day opens with a real painting, never pictures from the book: Ivan Shishkin’s snowy pine forest (1890), Pieter Bruegel’s Hunters in the Snow (1565), John Atkinson Grimshaw’s lamp-lit street in Leeds, England (1881), and a print of reindeer sledges crossing Lapland (1827). Your student looks first, then taps to learn about it.

Your Student’s Writing

Writer’s Workshop
Wk 25 · Describe a fantasy settingWk 26 · Explain a character’s choice

A new kind of writing starts this week: a literary response, writing that says what you think about a book and proves it with details from the book. The topic is the setting of Narnia. Day 1: brainstorm details from Chapter 1. Day 2: draft an opening sentence that names the book and the author. Day 3: add evidence, a short quote and what it shows. Day 4: write a closing sentence.

An example writer named Clara shows each step first.

Ask at Home

  1. What did Lucy feel and hear when she walked into the wardrobe?Listen for: something crunching under her feet, something soft and powdery and cold, branches instead of coats.
  2. Why didn’t the others believe Lucy?Listen for: she was gone only a moment, and the wardrobe looked ordinary when they checked.
  3. What does tele mean? Can you name a tele word?Listen for: far; telephone, television, telescope.
  4. Say “sofa” slowly. Which part is lazy?Listen for: the end, “uh,” spelled with an a.

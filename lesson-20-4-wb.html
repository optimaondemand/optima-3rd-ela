When you meet a word you don't know, use these three steps in order:

📌 Let's try it together: "barnacle"
1
Read the Sentence
Find the word in the book. What clues does the sentence give you?
"I can't have you sticking to me like a barnacle," said Jemmy.
Barnacles are tiny sea creatures that stick permanently to ships. Jemmy means the prince won't stop following him.
2
Look at the Word Parts
Do you see a root, prefix, or suffix you recognize?
eaves- + drop
Eaves are the edges of a roof. Long ago, people would stand under the eaves to listen to people inside through the windows. So eavesdrop = to listen secretly, like someone hiding under the eaves.
3

🔠 Spelling: r-Controlled Syllables (Day 4: Review + Explain)
Discover the pattern.

Sort and then write: explain the r-controlled pattern in your own words.

Group A — divided like this

rab·bit · gar·den · bas·ket · sis·ter

Group B — same pattern

tun·nel · ham·per · win·ter · pic·nic

🔍 What do you notice?

Look at the letters around the dot in each word. Count: vowel, consonant, consonant, vowel. That's the VCCV pattern.

Where does the dot fall — before, between, or after the two consonants?

Can you say the rule in your own words?

When a word has the VCCV pattern (vowel–consonant–consonant–vowel), you divide it between the two consonants.

This helps you read long words like tunnel (tun·nel) and basket (bas·ket).

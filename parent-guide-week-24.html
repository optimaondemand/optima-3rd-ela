This Week at a Glance

This is the second week of true articles about the Middle Ages. Each lesson has one short article, right on the page, so there is no book to have nearby. Day 1 is about how castles were built. Day 2 follows a boy on the long road to becoming a knight. Day 3 visits a loud, busy, smelly medieval town. Day 4 asks what stories like The Whipping Boy get right about the Middle Ages, and what they make up. Your student also finishes and publishes the informational paragraph started last week.

Reading skill
Cause and effect. A cause is why something happens. The effect is what happens. Castles had thick walls because enemies attacked.
Big idea
Real or imagined? Stories borrow from history but add imagination. What was the real Middle Ages like?

Word parts
The root script or scribe means write. One root, two spellings.script · describe · manuscript · inscription
Spelling
Four endings, four jobs: -ful means full of, -less means without, -ness names a thing, -ly tells how.hopeful · hopeless · kindness · quickly
Grammar
A paragraph has one main idea, often in the first sentence. The other sentences are details that support it. A sentence that doesn’t is off the topic.Castles were built to be hard to attack. The walls were thick enough to stop arrows.

New in the Lessons

🧺 Market Day
This week’s word game is a medieval market square. Each stall sells one kind of word: words with the root script or scribe on Day 1, and words with -ful, -less, -ness or -ly after that. Your student reads the word on the basket and taps the stall that sells it. Each right answer brings a customer, and by Day 4 the square is full.
🎬 Opti’s videos
Every Word Study activity, all four days, starts with a short video where Opti shows how it works. On Day 2, Opti also shows how to do Read Like a Writer with a different sentence from the knights article.
⭐ Job Spotlight
Each day opens the Reading tab with a real medieval job: a stonemason, a squire, a barber-surgeon, and a scribe.
🏮 Diligence Watch
After reading, your student decides if three facts from the article show someone working hard: Yes or Not yet.

Each day opens with a real painting: Jean Fouquet’s builders and their wooden crane (about 1470), two knights jousting in a book of songs (about 1305–1315), a busy town street painted in Siena, Italy (1338–1339), and a storybook knight painted in 1901, about 600 years after the Middle Ages. Your student looks first, then taps to learn about it.

Ask at Home

  1. Why were castle walls so thick? Can you say it as a because sentence?Listen for: because enemies attacked, or to stop arrows.
  2. What were the steps to becoming a knight?Listen for: page, then squire, then knight.
  3. What does -ness do to a word? What does -ly do?Listen for: -ness names a thing (kindness), -ly tells how (quickly).
  4. What is one thing stories about knights get right, and one thing they change?Listen for: castles and kings were real; perfect, always-honorable knights were more of an ideal.

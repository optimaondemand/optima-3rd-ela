Reading passage (the 24.1 article, “How Castles Were Built”)

Building a medieval castle was one of the biggest construction projects of its time. A large stone castle could take many years to finish, and some took more than thirty. Hundreds of workers — stonemasons, carpenters, blacksmiths, and laborers — worked on the site every day.
Castles were built for defense. The walls were thick — often ten feet or more — to withstand attack. A deep ditch called a moat was often dug around the outside and filled with water. A drawbridge could be raised to block the entrance. Every part of the castle’s design was meant to make it as difficult as possible for an enemy to get inside.

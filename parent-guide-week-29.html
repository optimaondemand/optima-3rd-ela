This Week at a Glance

In Chapter 13, Aslan’s rescuers save Edmund from the Witch, and Aslan tells the others there is no need to talk about what is past. Then the Witch comes to claim Edmund under the Deep Magic, an old law that says every traitor belongs to her. Aslan talks with her alone, and she gives up her claim. In Chapter 14 we learn the price: Aslan gives his own life in Edmund’s place. Chapter 15 begins on that sad night and ends at sunrise, when the Stone Table cracks and Aslan is alive again, because of a deeper magic the Witch did not know. Day 1 reads Chapter 13, Day 2 Chapter 14, Day 3 Chapter 15, and Day 4 looks back at the whole book so far.

Reading skill
Explaining the theme. A theme is the big lesson a story teaches, said as a full sentence. A topic is one word, like loyalty. Good readers back up a theme with details from the beginning, the middle and the end.
Big idea
What is the most important proof for your theme across the whole book? Edmund’s betrayal, his rescue and forgiveness, and Aslan’s choice all point to the same big lesson.

“Though the Witch knew the Deep Magic, there is a magic deeper still which she did not know.”Aslan, Chapter 15

Words to Know

Use them at home!
traitorsomeone who betrays the trust of their own side
atoneto make up for a wrong you have done
sacrificegiving up something precious for something or someone more important
resurrectioncoming back to life after death
Word parts
The root ject means throw. The part in front tells which way.reject (back) · inject (into) · eject (out) · project (forward)
Spelling
Long words are often a root with parts added to the front, the end, or both. Find the root first.un + expect + ed = unexpected
Grammar
In dialogue, quotation marks go around the exact words, the first word gets a capital, and the comma or period goes inside the closing mark.“Let him approach,” said Aslan.

New in the Lessons

🪨 Skipping Stones
This week’s word game is a river at a shallow crossing. On Days 1 and 2 your student reads a sentence with a ject word and taps which way something is thrown: back, into, out or forward. On Days 3 and 4 they find the root in the middle of a long word: ject, script or scribe, spect, or port. Each right answer skips a stone into the river, and by Day 4 the stepping stones fill the crossing.

After reading, your student decides if three moments show someone being honest: Yes or Not yet. This week includes Edmund saying “I’m sorry,” Aslan refusing to make a promise he may not keep, and the Witch hiding by making herself look like a rock.

Each day opens with real art, never pictures from the book: Caspar David Friedrich’s Dolmen in the Snow (1807) and Moonrise over the Sea (1822), Claude Monet’s Impression, Sunrise (1872), and Rosa Bonheur’s Portrait of a Lion (1879). Your student looks first, then taps to learn about it.

Good to Know

This is the hardest week of the book. At the start of Chapter 13, the Witch has Edmund tied to a tree and gets ready to kill him, but Aslan’s rescuers arrive in time. In Chapter 14, Aslan dies. He walks to the Stone Table at night and lets the Witch and her creatures tie him up, shave his mane, make fun of him, and then the Witch kills him with a knife, in Edmund’s place. Lucy and Susan watch from the bushes and cover their eyes at the moment he dies. Chapter 15 begins with the girls crying beside him through the night. At sunrise the Stone Table cracks, and Aslan is alive again.

The lessons keep all of this gentle: Opti tells students before Chapter 14 that it is the saddest chapter, that it is okay to feel sad, and that the story is not over yet. Your student may want to read Chapter 14 with you, or to know ahead of time that Aslan comes back to life in Chapter 15. It is fine to tell them. The unit guide has more about this part of the book.

Writer’s Workshop
Wk 28 · Add a symbol paragraphWk 29 · A literary response on theme

This week your student writes a literary response about the story’s theme. Day 1: brainstorm two or three possible themes, each as a full sentence that starts “The story shows that…”. Day 2: draft the response with proof from two parts of the story. Day 3: revise by adding two sentences of proof from Chapter 15. Day 4: finish the whole response, with proof from three different chapters and a closing sentence.

Ask at Home

  1. What is the difference between a topic and a theme?Listen for: a topic is a word, like loyalty; a theme is a full sentence about it, like “The story shows that…”.
  2. What did Aslan say to the others about Edmund after the rescue?Listen for: there is no need to talk about what is past; they forgave him.
  3. What was the Deeper Magic the Witch did not know?Listen for: when someone who did nothing wrong gives his life for a traitor, death works backwards.
  4. Where does the comma go in “Let him approach, said Aslan”?Listen for: inside the closing quotation mark, after approach.

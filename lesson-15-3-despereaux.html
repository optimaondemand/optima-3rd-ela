🌱 Morphology: The Suffix -ness
A suffix that turns an adjective into a noun

When we add -ness to an adjective, it becomes a noun that names a quality or state. Kate DiCamillo uses -ness words throughout The Tale of Despereaux to describe emotions and places.

📌 Examples from the Novel

darkness — base word dark + -ness = the state of being dark

sadness — base word sad + -ness = the quality of being sad

kindness — base word kind + -ness = the state of being kind

brightness — base word bright + -ness = the quality of being bright

🧩

Practice: Build It! Type the base word that makes each -ing word.

→ shining (the sun was shining)
→ staring (he was staring at the light)
→ sniffing (she was sniffing)
→ holding (he was holding a handkerchief)

✏️ Grammar: Pronouns: He, She, It, They
Learn it, spot it, explain it.

A pronoun takes the place of a noun so we do not have to repeat the same word over and over. The pronouns he, she, it, they replace nouns in a sentence.

👉 From the novel
The mouse mother, whose name was Antoinette, looked at her reflection.

He = Roscuro (replaces the noun so we do not say "Roscuro" again)

👉 Another example
His sister Merlot and his brother Furlough stared at Despereaux.

She = the Princess Pea; they = Roscuro and Gregory; it = the candle

🎯

Practice: Spot the Proper Noun! Tap the proper noun in each sentence.

He looked up at the light.
Gregory held it up high.
The princess she did not know.
The rats they lived in darkness.

🔠 Spelling: "au" vs. "aw"
Learn the rule, sort the words, explain it.

Both "au" and "aw" make the /ô/ sound. Here's the pattern:

📌 The Rule

"au" usually appears in the middle of a word: pause, August, fault, automobile

"aw" usually appears at the end or before n or l: flaw, straw, law, lawn, hawk

🎮

Practice: Quick Sort! Tap each word, then tap the rule it follows.

shining snapped staring stopped named wrapped waved nodding
Spelled with "aw"
Spelled with "au"
🪶 Show What You Know
In your own words, explain when to use "au" and when to use "aw" in words with the /ô/ sound.

This Week at a Glance

This is the last week of the year, and it is built to be lighter and fun: a reward for a year of hard work. Your student learns the 7 Pillars of Virtue (honesty, respect, courtesy, responsibility, courage, service and perseverance), chooses one character from this year’s books (Flat Stanley, The Tale of Despereaux, The Whipping Boy or The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe), and writes a short response about two pillars that character shows. On Day 4 your student shares it out loud, looks back at the whole year, and saves a certificate.

Reading skill
Look back at how your reading has grown. Name one skill that was hard in the fall and is easy now.
Big idea
What does it mean to have good character, and which character from our books best shows it?

“But they can try.”Mrs. Lambchop, Flat Stanley

Words to Know

Use them at home!
integritydoing the right thing even when no one is watching
empathyunderstanding and sharing the feelings of another person
resiliencethe ability to keep going after something hard
perseverancecontinuing to try even when things are hard
Word parts
The word parts inside the pillar words: -y (being), -ous (full of), -ance, and re-.honest + y · courage + ous · persevere + ance
Spelling
Find the base word first, then the rule: drop the silent e, double the last letter, change y to i, or add -es.hope → hoping · hop → hopping · cry → cried · box → boxes
Grammar
A final editing review: capitals, apostrophes, commas in a list and before and, and irregular past forms.lucy goed to narnia → Lucy went to Narnia

New in the Lessons

🏛️ Raise the 7 Pillars
This week’s word game: every right answer raises the stone pillars of a temple a little more. Days 1 and 2: what a word with a prefix or suffix means. Days 3 and 4: which spelling rule a word used. On Day 4 the last pillar stands and the roof goes on.
🏅 Bookshelf Awards
On Day 3 your student gives ribbons (Bravest, Most Honest, Kindest, Biggest Change) to characters from this year’s books. Each ribbon shows a real moment from the book.
🗺️ Reading Road look-back + certificate
On Day 4 Opti walks the year’s Reading Road, one book at a time, and your student picks a favorite book. Then your student picks the pillar to take to 4th grade and saves a “Story Map Explorer” certificate as a picture.
🌍 World Notes + Pillar Watch
Each day opens with a real thing behind one of our books: the real Flat Stanley Project (1995), Prince Edward’s best friend Barnaby Fitzpatrick (stories say he was a whipping boy; historians have no proof), where the word courtesy comes from, and the real Lewis family wardrobe at Wheaton College. After reading, the Pillar Watch asks if three book moments show a pillar: Yes or Not yet.

Opti’s short Word Study videos are silent with captions for now. Each day opens with real art: Frederic Edwin Church’s The Parthenon (1871), Hans Holbein’s Edward VI as a Child (about 1538), Edmund Blair Leighton’s God Speed! (1900), and Jean-Honoré Fragonard’s A Young Girl Reading (about 1769).

Your Student’s Writing

Writer’s Workshop
Wk 31 · Comparison response, publishedWk 32 · Culmination response: one character and two pillars

The last piece of writing of the year is short on purpose: an opening with a claim (the character, the book, and the pillar that matters most), two body paragraphs (a pillar, a real example from the book, and what it shows), and a closing. The writing lives on the Reading tab, so nothing is written twice. On Day 4 your student gives it a title and shares it out loud: a 1-minute video for Canvas, or reading it to someone at home, with one picture or object to show.

An example writer named Clara shows each step first, writing about Edmund.

Ask at Home

  1. What are the 7 pillars? Which one is hardest for you?Listen for: honesty, respect, courtesy, responsibility, courage, service, perseverance.
  2. Which character did you choose? Which two pillars do they show?Listen for: a real moment from the book for each pillar.
  3. Who won your Bookshelf Awards?Listen for: a reason, like Despereaux going into the dungeon or Lucy telling the truth about Narnia.
  4. What can you do as a reader now that was hard in the fall?Listen for: one skill, and a time they used it on their own.

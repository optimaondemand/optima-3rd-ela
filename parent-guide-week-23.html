This Week at a Glance

Your student just finished The Whipping Boy, a made-up story set long ago. This week and next, your student reads true articles about real life in the Middle Ages. Each lesson has one short article, right on the page, so there is no book to have nearby. Day 1 visits a castle. Day 2 is about how children learned a job by working. Day 3 explains the strict rules of a royal court. Day 4 compares what rich and poor people ate.

Reading skill
Central idea. The central idea is the most important point an author makes. A detail is one fact that helps prove it.
Big idea
Real life long ago. What was it really like to live in a castle, learn a trade, or sit at a king’s table?

“A medieval castle was not just a home — it was a fortress, a workplace, and a whole community.”Life in a Medieval Castle, Day 1

Words to Know

Use them at home!
fortressa large, strong building built to resist attack
keepthe strongest, innermost tower of a castle
servanta person who works in someone else’s household
pagea young boy in training at a noble household, learning to become a knight
apprenticea person learning a trade by working under an expert
craftsmana skilled worker who makes things by hand
pottagea thick soup of vegetables and grains
embroiderto decorate cloth with needle and thread
protocolofficial rules for how to behave in formal situations
ranka person’s position or level of importance
noblea person of high birth, below a king or queen
courteouspolite, respectful and well-mannered
feasta large, fancy meal for a celebration
coarserough in texture
pewtera dull grey metal used for plates and cups
Word parts
The word part trans means across. Careful: some words only start with t-r-a-n, like tranquil (calm).transport · transform · translate · transfer
Spelling
VCCV means vowel, consonant, consonant, vowel. Split the word between the two consonants.bas · ket · tun · nel · nap · kin
Grammar
Names of people, places and titles get capital letters. Small words like the, of and in stay small inside a name or title, unless they come first.Queen Eleanor lived near the Tower of London.

New in the Lessons

🖌️ Illuminate the Page
This week’s word game. Long ago, books were written and painted by hand. Each right answer paints one part of a medieval book page: the big gold capital letter on Day 1, the border on Day 2, a little castle picture on Day 3, and the lines of writing on Day 4.

⭐ Job Spotlight
Each day opens the Reading tab with a real medieval job: a page, an apprentice, a herald, and a castle cook.
🏮 Diligence Watch
After reading, your student decides if three facts from the article show someone working hard: Yes or Not yet. Diligence means working hard and carefully.

Each day opens with a real painting from the Middle Ages: a French castle above a grape harvest, a baker at his oven in Nuremberg, a king’s feast in Paris in 1378, and an English lord at dinner. Your student looks first, then taps to learn about it.

Your Student’s Writing

Writer’s Workshop
Wk 23 · Choose & take notesWk 24 · Draft, revise & publish

This week your student starts an informational paragraph about one part of medieval life. Day 1: choose a topic that is not too big. Day 2: write a research question and take notes from two articles. Day 3: plan the paragraph (topic sentence, two details, closing). Day 4: write the topic sentence and at least one detail. Week 24 finishes it.

Ask at Home

  1. What was the central idea of today’s article? What is one detail that proves it?Listen for: one big point, not just a fact, and a fact that backs it up.
  2. How did children learn a job in the Middle Ages?Listen for: by working, often as an apprentice to a master.
  3. What does trans mean? Can you name a word with it?Listen for: across, and a word like transport or translate.
  4. What topic are you writing about? What is your research question?Listen for: one small part of medieval life, and a question that starts with What, How or Why.

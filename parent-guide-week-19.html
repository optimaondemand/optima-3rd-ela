This Week at a Glance

Prince Brat and Jemmy have run away from the castle, and the outlaws Hold-Your-Nose Billy and Cutwater have caught them. When the outlaws find out that Jemmy can read and write but the prince cannot, they decide Jemmy must be the prince. Jemmy plays along. This week your student keeps asking why: why each boy wants to get away, why Jemmy helps a prince who has been cruel to him, and why the prince refuses to go home when he has the chance.

Reading skill
Think about why characters do what they do: what each one wants, and what he does to get it.
Big idea
Is Jemmy starting to change how he feels about the prince?

“It’s not me you think you’re protecting, is it?”Jemmy, The Whipping Boy

Word parts
The root port means “carry.”transport · import · export · report · portable
Spelling
Consonant-le syllables: the consonant before -le joins it to make the last syllable.ta·ble · can·dle · sim·ple
Grammar
Compound sentences join two complete thoughts with a comma and a joining word.Jemmy wrote the note, and Billy watched.

New in the Lessons

💰 Fill the Wagon
This week’s word game is the outlaws’ wagon. In Chapter 8, Jemmy tells Billy to ask the king for a whole wagonload of gold and jewels. Your student sorts one thing at a time into the right sack, and every right answer drops a coin or a jewel into the wagon. Day 1: what a port word means. Day 2: is it a compound sentence? Day 3: does it end in consonant-le? Day 4: all three. By Day 4 the wagon is full.
🔄 Read It Backward
On Day 2, your student tries Billy’s test from Chapter 9: Billy makes Jemmy read the ransom note backward, from the last word to the first. Your student reads three short notes backward out loud, then puts the words in the right order.
🎬 Opti’s videos
Every Word Study activity starts with a short video where Opti, the owl guide, shows how it works. On Day 2, Opti also shows Read Like a Writer with a different sentence from the book.
🦁 Courage Watch
After reading, your student decides if three moments from the chapters show courage: Yes or Not yet.

Each day opens with real art: Ivan Shishkin’s hut in a forest, a 1400s painting of the scribe Jean Miélot at his desk, George Stubbs’s horse Whistlejacket (about 1762), and Johann Adam Delsenbach’s Imperial Crown (1751).

Your Student’s Opinion Writing

Writer’s Workshop
Wk 18 · Choose a claimWk 19 · Add reasonsWk 20 · DraftWk 21 · Revise & publishWk 22 · Compare & present

Your student is building an opinion paragraph about a character in The Whipping Boy. Day 1: think about the other side (what would someone who disagrees say?). Day 2: read the draft aloud to see if it flows. Day 3: write a conclusion sentence. Day 4: write the whole paragraph with all five parts: claim, reason, example, the other side, and conclusion.

An example writer named Clara shows each step first. Her claim: Prince Brat is the most unfair character, because he uses his power to hurt others.

Ask at Home

  1. Why does Billy think Jemmy is the prince?Listen for: Jemmy can read and write, and the real prince can’t.
  2. Why does Jemmy play along?Listen for: a reason from the book, in your student’s own words.
  3. Why does Jemmy want a whole wagonload of gold and jewels?Listen for: what your student thinks Jemmy is planning, with a detail from Chapter 8.
  4. Is Jemmy starting to feel differently about the prince? How can you tell?Listen for: an action, like Jemmy stepping in when Billy goes to whip the prince.

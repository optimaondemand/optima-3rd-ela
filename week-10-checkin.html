Reading: How Setting Affects Characters
3 points
The dungeon was always cold and always dark. It smelled of damp stone and old secrets. Miggery Sow had lived there long enough that she sometimes forgot there was a world above — a world with sunlight, warm bread, and the sound of music drifting through open windows.
— Adapted from The Tale of Despereaux, Book Three, Ch. 31–33
Cite a Detail 1 pt
10What is the dungeon like? Use two details from the passage in your answer.
Setting Affects Character 1 pt
11How does living in the dungeon affect Miggery Sow? What does she forget or not know about? Use the passage to support your answer.
Infer 1 pt
12How do you think the dungeon setting makes Miggery Sow feel about being a princess? Use details from the passage and the story to explain.

Writing & RWM Transfer
2 points
Read Like a Writer — Mimic Sentence 1 pt
Model Sentence (Sensory Details to Build Setting)
"The dungeon smelled of wet stone and old darkness, and the drip of water echoed off the walls like a slow, sad clock."
13Notice how the author uses sensory details — smell, sound — to make the dungeon feel real. Write your own sentence describing a setting using at least two senses (sight, sound, smell, touch, or taste).
Tip: Pick a place from your story or imagination. What would you see, hear, and smell there?
Writing Arc — Revise Setting and Description 1 pt

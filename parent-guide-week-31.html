This Week at a Glance

The novels are finished, and this is an extension week. Your student reads two short texts about courage, both written for Optima Academy Online. Text 1, “What Is Courage?” is informational: it explains that courage is not the same as having no fear, but choosing to act anyway because it matters. Text 2, “The Choice at the Bridge,” is a made-up story about a girl named Mira who stands up for a smaller boy on an old, narrow bridge, even though she is scared. All week your student compares how the two authors write about the same topic, and builds a comparison response.

Reading skill
Compare how two authors write about the same topic. One text explains courage with facts and ideas. The other shows it through a character’s actions.
Big idea
What does it mean to be truly courageous? On Day 4, your student thinks back over every book read this year.

“I just decided that he mattered more than my fear did.”Mira, Text 2

New in the Lessons

🚣 Row to the Wreck
This week’s word game follows a real rescue. Each right answer is one pull on the oars as Grace Darling and her father row through the storm to a wrecked ship. On Days 1 and 2 your student picks what a word with a prefix or suffix means. On Days 3 and 4 your student counts the beats (syllables) in long words. By Day 4 the boat reaches the wreck.
🎬 Opti’s videos
Every Word Study activity, all four days, starts with a short video where Opti shows how it works. On Day 2, Opti also shows Read Like a Writer with a different sentence from Text 2. The videos are silent with captions for now.
🌍 World Notes
Each day opens the Reading tab with a real story of courage: Grace Darling, who in 1838, at age 22, rowed with her father from the Longstone Lighthouse to save nine people from a shipwreck; how a lighthouse works; rowing through rough water; and the stone monument in Bamburgh, England, that shows her holding an oar.
🦁 Courage Watch
After reading, your student decides if three moments show someone being brave: Yes or Not yet. For example: Mira walks onto the bridge even though her feet want to turn around (Yes), or someone who is never scared of anything (Not yet: Text 1 says brave people feel fear and act anyway).

Each day opens with real art: Thomas Musgrave Joy’s portrait of Grace Darling (1839), Camille Corot’s painting of the old bridge at Narni in Italy (1827), Peder Balke’s lighthouse on the coast of Norway (about 1855), and Henry Perlee Parker’s painting of Grace and her father rowing to the rescue.

This week your student writes a comparison response. Day 1: plan what Text 1 says courage is, a character who shows it, and a claim. Day 2: draft the opening, naming both texts and what they agree on. Day 3: draft one paragraph for each text, plus a sentence that connects them. Day 4: write the closing and put the whole response together.

💬

Ask at Home

  1. What does Text 1 say courage is?Listen for: not having no fear, but choosing to act anyway because it is important or right; something you practice, one choice at a time.
  2. How does the author show that Mira is scared, without using the word scared?Listen for: her heart hammered, her feet wanted to turn around, she took a breath.
  3. How are the two texts alike? How are they different?Listen for: both say courage means acting even when afraid; one explains it, the other shows it in a story.
  4. Which character from this year’s books showed the most courage?Listen for: a character and one thing they did, like Despereaux going into the dungeon or Lucy telling the truth about Narnia.

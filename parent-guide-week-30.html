This Week at a Glance

This is the last week of the book. In Chapter 16, Aslan breathes on the Witch’s stone statues and they come back to life, then leads them to help Peter’s army. In Chapter 17, the battle ends, Lucy’s cordial heals Edmund, and the four children are crowned kings and queens at Cair Paravel. Years later, hunting the White Stag, they find the lamp-post and tumble back out of the wardrobe into the Professor’s house, on the same day and at the same hour they left. Day 1 reads Chapters 16 and 17. Days 2, 3 and 4 look back over the whole book.

Reading skill
How the ending shows the theme. The end of a book often answers a question the beginning asked. Edmund began as a traitor and ends as King Edmund the Just.
Big idea
How would you explain what this book is really about to someone who has never read it? Your student finishes a whole literary response on theme this week.

“Once a King in Narnia, always a King in Narnia.”the Professor, Chapter 17

New in the Lessons

🗿 Statues Come Alive
This week’s word game is the Witch’s courtyard from Chapter 16. On Days 1 and 2 your student picks what a word built from one of the year’s roots means. On Days 3 and 4 they name the syllable type of a bold part of a word. Each right answer turns a stone statue to full color, and by Day 4 the courtyard “looked more like a zoo.”
🎬 Opti’s videos
Every Word Study activity, all four days, starts with a short video where Opti shows how it works. On Day 2, Opti also shows Read Like a Writer with a different sentence from Chapter 16. The videos have captions for now; Opti’s voice will be added later.
🌍 World Notes
Each day opens the Reading tab with a real thing behind the story: real coronations, the bronze lions of Trafalgar Square in London, mermaids in old sailors’ stories, and the white stag of old legends.
🏮 Truth Watch
After reading, your student decides if three moments show someone being honest: Yes or Not yet. This week looks back over the whole book, from Edmund pretending he never went to Narnia to the children telling the Professor the whole story.

Each day opens with real art, never pictures from the book: George Hayter’s painting of Queen Victoria’s coronation (1838), an old color photograph of the lions in Trafalgar Square, John Varley’s Bamburgh Castle, a castle above the sea, and the white deer from the Wilton Diptych, painted more than 600 years ago. Your student looks first, then taps to learn about it.

Good to Know

Chapter 16 ends with a battle. Aslan leaps on the White Witch, and the Witch is killed; most of her army is killed or runs away. Edmund is badly wounded in the fighting, but Lucy’s cordial heals him, and Aslan makes him a knight. The book keeps the battle short and not detailed. If your student was upset by last week’s chapters, this week brings the happy ending: Aslan alive, Edmund forgiven and healed, and all four children crowned.

Ask at Home

  1. How is Edmund different at the end of the book?Listen for: he fought bravely and broke the Witch’s wand; he was forgiven, healed and crowned King Edmund the Just.
  2. What happened when the four kings and queens followed the White Stag?Listen for: they found the lamp-post and came back out of the wardrobe, on the same day and at the same hour.
  3. What is the theme of the book, in one sentence?Listen for: a full sentence such as “The story shows that forgiveness can change someone,” with a detail to back it up.
  4. What does the root port mean? Can you name a word with it?Listen for: carry; transport, report, portable.

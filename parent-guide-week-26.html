This Week at a Glance

In Chapter 4, Edmund rides on the Queen’s sledge and eats enchanted Turkish Delight. The more he eats, the more he wants, and he promises to bring his brother and sisters to her. Back home in Chapter 5, he tells Peter and Susan that Lucy’s story was only a game, and the Professor helps Peter and Susan think it through. In Chapter 6, all four children step into Narnia, find Mr. Tumnus’s cave broken and empty, and choose to go on. Day 1 reads Chapter 4, Day 2 Chapter 5, Day 3 Chapter 6, and Day 4 looks back at all three.

Reading skill
Choices and what they cost. Good readers find the moment a character decides something, and follow what happens because of it.
Big idea
Why do people make choices they know are wrong? Is Edmund to blame, or the enchanted sweets?

Words to Know

Use them at home!
enchantedunder a magic spell; bewitched
dominionsthe lands ruled by someone
wretchedvery unhappy or miserable
treacherybetraying someone’s trust in a serious way
Word parts
The root form means shape.form · uniform · reform · transform
Spelling
Long school words are read in beats. Every beat has one vowel sound, so count sounds, not letters.de·tail · ev·i·dence · in·for·ma·tion
Grammar
Verb tense consistency: once a story starts in the past, every verb stays in the past.Edmund sat on the sledge and ate the sweets.

New in the Lessons

🍬 The Turkish Delight Box
This week’s word game is the Queen’s box of Turkish Delight. On Days 1 and 3 your student claps long school words and taps how many beats they have. On Days 2 and 4 they read a sentence and decide if its verbs stay in one time. Each right answer adds a sweet, and by Day 4 the box is full.
🎬 Opti’s videos
Every Word Study activity, all four days, starts with a short video where Opti shows how it works. On Day 2, Opti also shows Read Like a Writer with a different sentence from Chapter 5. The videos have captions for now; Opti’s voice will be added later.
🌍 World Notes
Each day opens the Reading tab with a real thing behind the story: Turkish delight (a real candy), old country houses that visitors come to see, robins in winter, and Arctic explorers.
🏮 Truth Watch
After reading, your student decides if three moments show someone being honest: Yes or Not yet. This week there is a lot to notice: Edmund’s secret, his lie, and the people who tell the truth.

Each day opens with a real painting, never pictures from the book: Hendrick Avercamp’s skaters on a frozen river (about 1608), John Constable’s English country house (1816), robins from a bird book (1837), and Ivan Shishkin’s lone snowy pine (1891). Your student looks first, then taps to learn about it.

Your Student’s Writing

Writer’s Workshop
Wk 25 · Describe a fantasy settingWk 26 · Explain a character’s choice

This week’s literary response explains a character’s choice in three parts: the choice, the reason, and the consequence. Day 1: draft the paragraph. Day 2: revise with connecting words like because and as a result. Day 3: put it all together with an opening and a closing. Day 4: polish by swapping a weak word for a stronger one.

An example writer named Clara shows each step first.

Ask at Home

  1. What did Edmund promise the Queen, and why?Listen for: to bring his brother and sisters, so he could have more Turkish Delight.
  2. What did Edmund say about Narnia when Peter asked? How did Lucy feel?Listen for: that it was only a game; Lucy rushed out of the room.
  3. Is Edmund to blame, or the enchanted sweets?Listen for: any answer with a reason from the book.
  4. Clap “information.” How many beats?Listen for: four: in·for·ma·tion.

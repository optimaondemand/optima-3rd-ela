Gulliver's Stories — Part 4: The Journey Home
📅 Week 25 · Check-In

This week Gulliver finally returned home after all his strange journeys. You studied compound sentences, the prefix tele- (meaning "far"), and schwa sounds in longer words. Show what you know!

✎
Section 1: Grammar — Compound Sentences
3 points
1 Multiple Choice
Which sentence is a compound sentence?
  • A Gulliver returned home after many years away.
  • B He had seen so many strange lands, and he could not forget what he had learned.
  • C When Gulliver came home, he looked at everything differently.
2 Fix-It Sentence
The sentence below is a run-on. Rewrite it as a correct compound sentence using a comma and a coordinating conjunction.
"Gulliver walked through his hometown but he felt like a stranger, nothing looked the same to him anymore."
3 Write Your Own
Write your own compound sentence about Gulliver's return home. Use one of these joining words: and, but, or, so.
📖
Section 2: Morphology — Prefix tele- (far)
3 points
4 Prefix Meaning
The prefix tele- means "far." Think about the word telescope. What does telescope mean? How does knowing tele- help you figure it out?
5 Multiple Choice — Word in Context
"Before phones were invented, people sent messages over long distances using a telegraph — it allowed writing to travel far away quickly."
Original passage for class
What does telegraph most likely mean?
  • A A machine that moves things from place to place
  • B A way to send written messages over a great distance
  • C A tool for measuring how far away something is
6 Apply It
Think of one more word with the prefix tele- (like television, telephone, or teleport). Write it and explain how "far" connects to its meaning.
✍
Section 3: Spelling — Schwa in Longer Words
3 points
7 Find the Schwa
The schwa is the "uh" sound a vowel makes in an unstressed syllable (like the a in sofa or the e in happen). Read each word and write the syllable that contains the schwa sound.
banana pencil pilot balloon button
8 Explain the Trick
Why can the schwa sound be tricky for spellers? Explain in your own words, and give one example of a word where the schwa might fool you.
9 Spot the Error
One word below is misspelled. Find it and write the correct spelling.
balloon pencal button

Section 4: Reading — Realistic vs. Imaginary Details
3 points
When Gulliver finally came home, he sat in his own chair and looked around his house. Everything was exactly the right size — the chairs, the table, the doorways — and yet it all felt somehow strange to him. He had been to places where people were the size of his thumb and places where people were as tall as trees, and now he was not sure what was real anymore.
Adapted from Gulliver's Stories, Part 4
10 Realistic vs. Imaginary
What details in this passage are realistic (could happen in real life)? What details remind us that Gulliver has been to imaginary places?
11 Explain the Character
Why does Gulliver feel strange being home, even though everything is normal? Use a detail from the passage to explain.
12 Compare
Compare Gulliver's home to one of the imaginary places he visited. How are they different? Use at least one detail from the passage and one detail from earlier in the book.

Section 5: Writing & Read-Write-Mean
2 points
13 Read-Write-Mean (RWM)
📚 Model Sentence
"His own house sat quietly on a real street in a real town — but after visiting lands no mapmaker had ever drawn, even an ordinary chair felt like something out of a story."
Notice how the author compares the real world with the imaginary worlds Gulliver visited. Write your own sentence that compares a real place with an imaginary one, using specific details from each.
14 Writing Arc — Revise and Publish
Look back at the paragraph you drafted last week (Week 24). Revise it by: adding one more specific detail, fixing any verb tense errors, and writing a stronger closing sentence. Write your revised paragraph here.
